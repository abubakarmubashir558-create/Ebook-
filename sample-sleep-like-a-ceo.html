Chapter 2: The first three Sleep Saboteurs

  1. The Blue Light Delusion: Your phone screen is a circadian disruptor. Blue light suppresses melatonin, telling your brain it's high noon when it's 11 PM.
  2. Thermal Inefficiency: Your body needs a 2-3 degree drop in core temperature to initiate deep sleep. If your room is over 68F (20C), you are fighting your biology.
  3. The Caffeine "Long Tail": Caffeine has a half-life of 6 hours. That 4 PM espresso is still blocking 25% of your adenosine receptors at midnight.

Chapter 3: The CEO Nightly Ritual

Peak performance requires a transition. You wouldn't launch a product without a checklist; don't launch your sleep without one.

The 3-2-1 Rule for Executive Rest

  • 3 Hours Before Bed: No more food. Let your digestion finish so your body can focus on cellular repair.
  • 2 Hours Before Bed: No more work. Close the laptop. No high-stress conversations.
  • 1 Hour Before Bed: No more screens. Dim the lights.

The 15-Minute "Shutdown" Sequence

  1. Cognitive Offloading (5 Mins): Journal for 5 minutes. Write down the top 3 priorities for tomorrow and any "open loops" in your mind. This clears the mental cache.
  2. The "Power Down" Environment (2 Mins): Set thermostat to 65-68F. Ensure total blackout (curtains or high-quality mask).
  3. Vagal Nerve Activation (8 Mins): Perform "Box Breathing" (4s inhale, 4s hold, 4s exhale, 4s hold) or the "4-7-8" technique.

Chapter 7: The Sleep Audit Scorecard

Before you chase supplements or gadgets, score the basics. Most sleep problems come from a small number of recurring leaks.

  • Bedtime consistency: Green = same window most nights; Yellow = varies by 30-60 minutes; Red = all over the place.
  • Caffeine cutoff: Green = before noon; Yellow = mid-afternoon; Red = after dinner.
  • Light exposure: Green = morning light + dim evenings; Yellow = some daylight; Red = bright screens in bed.
  • Room temperature: Green = cool and stable; Yellow = sometimes warm; Red = hot and stuffy.
  • Wind-down routine: Green = repeatable sequence; Yellow = inconsistent; Red = no routine.
Sample page 5 of 5

Chapter 8: Bedroom checklist

Your environment should make good sleep easier than bad sleep. Think of the bedroom like a recovery lab, not an entertainment center.

  • Blackout curtains or a true sleep mask
  • Cool room temperature
  • Phone charging outside arm's reach
  • Soft lighting after sunset
  • Clean sheets and low clutter
  • A repeatable bedtime cue
